Free self-guided walking tour of Barcelona's Gothic Quarter and El Born

By Mehmet Selim Çetin, developer of Guidera · Published 1 October 2026 · Last updated 1 October 2026 · 10 min read

This free self-guided walk through Barcelona's Gothic Quarter, El Born and La Rambla is about 5.2 km long, with about 1 hour 10 minutes of walking and 15 stops. It starts at Plaça de Catalunya and ends at the Columbus Monument. Every stop below has a short summary and a link to the source it is based on.

What does the route look like?

The route starts at Plaça de Catalunya, winds through the Gothic Quarter past the cathedral, the Pont del Bisbe, Plaça de Sant Jaume and Plaça del Rei, crosses into El Born for Santa Maria del Mar and the Born market, visits the Palau de la Música Catalana, returns through Plaça de Sant Felip Neri to La Rambla, and ends at the Columbus Monument by the port.

What will I see on this walk?

1. Plaça de Catalunya

Plaça de Catalunya is generally considered Barcelona's city centre, where the old city meets the 19th-century Eixample. It is known for its fountains and statues and the compass rose on its pavement, and major streets meet here, including Passeig de Gràcia and La Rambla.

Source: Wikipedia: Plaça de Catalunya

2. Cathedral of Barcelona

Barcelona Cathedral, the Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulalia, is the seat of the Archbishop of Barcelona. It was built from the 13th to the 15th centuries; the cloister, which encloses the Well of the Geese, was completed in 1448, and the neo-Gothic façade was added in the late 19th century.

Source: Wikipedia: Barcelona Cathedral

3. Pont del Bisbe

The Pont del Bisbe is the Flamboyant-style bridge across Carrer del Bisbe between the Palau de la Generalitat and the Cases dels Canonges. Although it looks medieval, it was built in 1928 by Joan Rubió.

Source: Wikipedia: Gothic Quarter, Barcelona

4. Plaça de Sant Jaume

Plaça de Sant Jaume is the administrative heart of both Barcelona and Catalonia: the Palace of the Generalitat of Catalonia and the City Hall face each other across it. It lies on the site of the centre of the Roman city of Barcino, where its main streets crossed and the forum stood.

Source: Wikipedia: Plaça Sant Jaume

5. Plaça del Rei

Plaça del Rei is a 14th-century medieval square surrounded by the Palau Reial Major with the Saló del Tinell, King Martin's Watchtower and the Royal Chapel of St Agatha. Casa Padellàs on its south side was moved here stone by stone in 1931 and has housed the Barcelona City History Museum (MUHBA) since 1943.

Source: Wikipedia: Plaça del Rei

6. Basilica of Santa Maria del Mar

Santa Maria del Mar was built between 1329 and 1383, when Catalonia was at the height of its maritime power. It is an outstanding example of Catalan Gothic with an unusual unity of style. Its construction was backed by the common people rather than the nobility.

Source: Wikipedia: Santa Maria del Mar, Barcelona

7. Fossar de les Moreres

The Fossar de les Moreres is a memorial square next to Santa Maria del Mar, built over the cemetery where defenders of the city were buried after the Siege of Barcelona in 1714. It has a torch with an eternal flame and a poem by Frederic Soler.

Source: Wikipedia: Fossar de les Moreres

8. El Born Centre de Cultura i Memòria

The Mercat del Born is a former public market built of iron between 1874 and 1878 to a design by Antoni Rovira i Trias. It is described as the largest covered square in Europe and marked the start of Modernisme in Catalan architecture. Since 2013 the building shows the excavated ruins beneath it to visitors, alongside a cultural centre and a museum about the War of the Spanish Succession.

Source: Wikipedia: Mercat del Born

9. Palau de la Música Catalana

The Palau de la Música Catalana is a concert hall in the Catalan modernista style, designed by Lluís Domènech i Montaner and built between 1905 and 1908 for the Orfeó Català choral society. It was inaugurated on 9 February 1908.

Source: Wikipedia: Palau de la Música Catalana

10. Plaça de Sant Felip Neri

Plaça de Sant Felip Neri is a small Gothic Quarter square named after its church, with an octagonal fountain in the middle. In the mid-20th century, Renaissance buildings from other parts of the city were moved here stone by stone.

Source: Wikipedia: Plaça de Sant Felip Neri

11. Mercat de la Boqueria

La Boqueria is a large public market with its entrance on La Rambla, near the Liceu opera house. Its first mention dates from 1217, when tables were set up near the old city gate to sell meat.

Source: Wikipedia: La Boqueria

12. La Rambla

La Rambla is a tree-lined pedestrian street running 1.2 km from Plaça de Catalunya to the Columbus Monument at the port, between the Barri Gòtic and El Raval. It can be very crowded in high season.

Source: Wikipedia: La Rambla, Barcelona

13. Plaça Reial

Plaça Reial is a square in the Barri Gòtic just off La Rambla, known for its restaurants, nightlife and open-air concerts during the La Mercè festival in September. It was built on the site of a Capuchin convent demolished in 1835.

Source: Wikipedia: Royal Square (Barcelona)

14. Palau Güell

Palau Güell is a mansion designed by Antoni Gaudí for the industrialist Eusebi Güell and built between 1886 and 1888 on Carrer Nou de la Rambla. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Works of Antoni Gaudí".

Source: Wikipedia: Palau Güell

15. Mirador de Colom (Columbus Monument)

The Columbus Monument at the lower end of La Rambla is 60 m tall and was built for the 1888 Universal Exposition of Barcelona. A 7.2 m bronze statue of Columbus stands on top of a 40 m Corinthian column.

Source: Wikipedia: Columbus Monument, Barcelona
