Why you can trust the stories

Checked before it's told.

AI can make things up. Guidera is built so it doesn't. Read the full story →

01

Research first

Before narrating, Guidera researches the place from Wikipedia, Wikidata, Wikivoyage, OpenStreetMap and official city websites.

02

Verify every claim

A verifier checks the story against those sources and removes anything they don't support.

03

Say less, not more

For little-known places, the guide says less rather than making things up.

Real example · Big Ben, London

One story, traced back to its sources

The story Guidera told

The name Big Ben actually belongs only to the Great Bell swinging in the dark of the belfry, not the stone tower itself.1 The tower was officially called the Clock Tower until 2012, when it was renamed the Elizabeth Tower for Queen Elizabeth II's Diamond Jubilee.2 Completed in 1859, the structure was designed in the Perpendicular Gothic style by Augustus Pugin and Charles Barry.3 It rises exactly three hundred and sixteen feet, concealing three hundred and thirty-four steps up to the bells.4 Cast by the Whitechapel Bell Foundry, that central bell weighs thirteen and a half long tons.5 Popular accounts credit its nickname to Sir Benjamin Hall, the large commissioner who oversaw the installation, while others point to heavyweight boxer Ben Caunt.6 Twice each day, its resonant toll is broadcast live across the country on BBC Radio 4.7 Today it forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site alongside the Palace of Westminster, which stretches out about one hundred and fifty metres to the south.8 Across the street, about one hundred and eighty metres to the south-west, lies St. Margaret's Church.9

What each sentence is based on

  1. The name Big Ben originally referred only to the Great Bell inside the tower.
    Source: Wikipedia, Wikivoyage
  2. The tower was known as the Clock Tower until 2012, when it was renamed the Elizabeth Tower for the Diamond Jubilee.
    Source: Wikipedia, Wikivoyage
  3. Completed in 1859, designed by Augustus Pugin and Charles Barry in the Perpendicular Gothic style.
    Source: Wikipedia
  4. 316 feet tall; 334 steps to the belfry.
    Source: Wikipedia
  5. The Great Bell weighs 13.5 long tons and was cast by the Whitechapel Bell Foundry.
    Source: Wikipedia
  6. The nickname is generally credited to Sir Benjamin Hall; some say boxer Ben Caunt.
    Source: Wikipedia
  7. The bell is broadcast live on BBC Radio 4 twice a day.
    Source: Wikipedia
  8. Part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Palace of Westminster: 146 m to the south.
    Source: Wikipedia; distance from map data
  9. St. Margaret's Church: 177 m to the south-west.
    Source: distance from map data

Left out on purpose: how the clock mechanism keeps time. The sources had no details on it, so the guide didn't narrate it.

Questions?

Is Guidera free?+

Yes, it’s free to download. The free tier gives you 5 AI stories a day (up to 20 by watching short ads), with banner ads. Premium is sold as passes, not a subscription: €1 for 3 days, €3 for a week or €7 for a month.

Does it work offline?+

Not yet. New stories need an internet connection.

Which languages are supported?+

9 languages, for both the app and the stories: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese (Brazil), Turkish and Simplified Chinese.

Is my location data private?+

Only the coordinates of the place you tap are sent to create a story, never your own location. There are no user accounts. See the Privacy Policy.
